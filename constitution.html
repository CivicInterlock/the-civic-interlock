HOW TO USE THIS TEMPLATE: This document is an open-source starting point to help local groups organize shared resources and clear up responsibilities. It isn't a rigid, top-down contract. Local participants are encouraged to take this text, adapt it to their own neighborhood's culture, and pair it with standard, common-sense safety practices for their area.

ARTICLE I: PURPOSE AND NATURE

Section 1.1 — Information Ledger Status

This group operates strictly as a private, voluntary information ledger and mutual credit record. It is not a bank, financial institution, money transmitter, depository, or commercial enterprise. It does not issue financial currency, take monetary deposits, or offer speculative investments.

Section 1.2 — Purpose

The sole purpose of this agreement is to provide a clear, peer-to-peer record for tracking community resource availability, voluntary service hours, shared equipment, and local mutual credit among participants.

Section 1.3 — Non-Extractive Charter

Operations are conducted on a mutual-benefit, non-profit basis. No speculative profits, yields, or private dividends accrue to any participant or steward.

ARTICLE II: RESOURCE CUSTODY & STEWARDSHIP

Section 2.1 — Shared Custody Model

Physical tools, equipment, supplies, and reserves contributed to the group operate under a clear division of custody and utility:

  1. Administrative Custody: Physical stewardship of shared goods is attached to designated volunteer roles (e.g., Quartermaster, Store Steward) rather than the individual personal property of the volunteer.
  2. Community Utility: The right to use and benefit from shared equipment remains with the active community members according to the transparent ledger rules.
Section 2.2 — Role-Based Responsibility

Volunteers serving in steward positions act as caretakers on behalf of the group. Personal assets of individual stewards remain distinct from shared community equipment, and stepping down from a volunteer role transfers stewardship duties directly to the incoming steward.

Section 2.3 — Voluntary Participation

All contributions of time, tools, or supplies to the store balance or treasury are voluntary. Participation does not constitute an investment contract or expectation of monetary return.

ARTICLE III: ACCOUNTING & RESERVES

Section 3.1 — Dual-Ledger Structure

To keep daily operations simple while protecting long-term reserves, the ledger uses two distinct balances: the Store Balance and the Treasury.

Section 3.2 — The Store Balance (Daily Flow)
  1. Definition: Represents active, short-term records generated by voluntary hours, local supply contributions, and day-to-day mutual aid.
  2. Purpose: Used for immediate operational needs, shop maintenance, tool sharing, and local pantry distribution.
  3. Governance: Managed through simple consensus among active local volunteers and daily stewards.
Section 3.3 — The Treasury (Capital & Equipment Reserves)
  1. Definition: Holds long-term community assets, physical equipment reserves, emergency supplies, and maintenance reserves.
  2. Purpose: To ensure long-term stability against supply disruptions or sudden operational costs.
  3. Governance: Allocations require formal agreement among stewards and open audit verification against physical inventory.

ARTICLE IV: OPEN GOVERNANCE & AUDITS

Section 4.1 — Open Ledger Requirement

Any active participant has the right to review the full, unedited ledger of inventory, store balances, and physical asset logs at any time. Transparent record-keeping is required for all active stewards.

Section 4.2 — Dissolution of Shared Assets

In the event that the local group dissolves, remaining physical assets and reserves shall not be liquidated for personal profit. Remaining supplies and tools shall be transferred to another local non-profit, community land trust, or open-source community group operating under similar principles.
