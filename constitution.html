CONSTITUTION AND ARTICLES OF ASSOCIATION

[NAME OF LOCAL GUILD] CIVIC INTERLOCK

ARTICLE I: DECLARATION OF NATURE AND INTENT

Section 1.1 — Pure Information Ledger Status

This Association operates exclusively as a private, decentralized information ledger and mutual credit registry. The Association is not a bank, financial institution, money transmitter, depository, broker-dealer, or commercial enterprise. It does not issue currency, hold deposits, or execute financial investments.

Section 1.2 — Purpose

The sole purpose of this Association is to provide a transparent, peer-to-peer record-keeping mechanism for tracking local resource availability, voluntary labor hours, physical equipment, and mutual community credit among its voluntary participants.

Section 1.3 — Non-Extractive Charter

The Association operates strictly on a non-extractive, mutual-benefit basis. No profit, yield, or speculative gain shall accrue to any individual participant, officer, or council member.

ARTICLE II: LEGAL SHIELDING AND SPLIT BAILMENT

Section 2.1 — Split Bailment Principle

All physical property, tools, land rights, reserves, and tangible assets contributed to or managed through the Association are held under a strict legal framework of Split Bailment:

  1. The Legal Title/Custody: Legal stewardship and administrative custody of Association assets are attached solely and exclusively to the Designated Council Seats (e.g., Seat A: Quartermaster, Seat B: Treasury Steward), as defined in Article IV, and not to the natural persons occupying those seats at any given time.
  2. The Beneficial Ownership: Beneficial interest and rights of physical utility remain vested entirely in the Active Community Membership as a collective whole, governed by the transparent ledger.
Section 2.2 — Immunity and Non-Transferability of Individual Liability

Because custody is attached to the Seat rather than the Individual:

  • No natural person occupying a Council Seat assumes personal financial, contractual, or tort liability for actions taken in good faith on behalf of the Association Seat.
  • Personal creditors, judgments, or tax obligations of an individual council member shall have zero claim or attachment against assets held in bailment by the Council Seat.
  • The vacation, resignation, or replacement of a council member transfers all custodial bailment duties automatically to the incoming seat occupant without requiring the re-execution or novation of underlying bailment contracts.
Section 2.3 — Voluntary Contribution and Non-Investment

Contributions made to the Store Balance or Treasury are voluntary contributions of labor, equipment, or resources. Participation does not constitute an investment contract, stock purchase, or expectation of monetary dividend.

ARTICLE III: ACCOUNTING, STORE BALANCE, AND TREASURY

Section 3.1 — Dual-Ledger Architecture

To maintain physical reality and operational velocity, the Association's ledger is divided into two distinct operational accounts: the Store Balance and the Treasury.

Section 3.2 — The Store Balance (Fluid Operations)
  1. Definition: The Store Balance represents the active, short-term operational ledger generated by voluntary labor, service hours, local resource contributions, and daily mutual aid.
  2. Purpose: Used exclusively for immediate operational needs, volunteer reimbursements, shop maintenance, local food or tool distribution, and short-term community builds.
  3. Governance: Dispersal from the Store Balance requires simple operational consensus from active volunteers and the designated operational stewards.
Section 3.3 — The Treasury (Fixed Capital & Reserves)
  1. Definition: The Treasury represents the long-term capital preservation reserve of the Association, holding major physical assets, land bailments, legal defense reserves, and emergency capital.
  2. Purpose: To anchor the long-term endurance of the Association against external economic shocks, legal interference, or environmental distress.
  3. Governance: Dispersal from the Treasury requires formal, supermajority consensus from the seated Council and full audit transparency against the physical ledger.

ARTICLE IV: GOVERNANCE AND COUNCIL SEATS

Section 4.1 — Council Seat Structure

Governance is executed by a Council composed of defined, functional Seats (e.g., Steward of the Ledger, Steward of Physical Assets, Steward of Legal Defense).

Section 4.2 — Duty of the Seat

Individuals occupying a Council Seat act strictly as fiduciaries and bailment stewards. They do not possess individual executive authority over the Association outside the explicit rules of the Ledger and this Constitution.

Section 4.3 — Open Audit Requirement

Any participant in good standing has the absolute right to inspect the complete, unedited ledger of the Store Balance, Treasury, and physical asset logs at any time. Secrecy in ledger entries constitutes immediate forfeiture of the responsible Council Seat.

ARTICLE V: DISSOLUTION AND PROTECTION OF COMMONS

Section 5.1 — Non-Distribution of Assets

In the event of the dissolution of the Association, no physical assets, Treasury reserves, or Store Balances shall be distributed to individual members, council occupants, or third-party corporate entities.

Section 5.2 — Return to the Local Commons

Upon dissolution, all physical assets held in bailment shall be transferred directly to another non-profit, open-source, or community-land trust operating under equivalent principles of local carrying capacity and non-extraction.

Structural Protections Summary:
  • Pre-empts Money-Transmission Laws: Strictly defined as a private information/record-keeping ledger.
  • Neutralizes Personal Risk: Decouples the individual from the position through Split Bailment.
  • Prevents Corruption: Strict non-distribution and open-audit requirements safeguard community reserves.
